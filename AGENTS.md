# AGENTS.md

Working notes for any agent (or person) picking up Marine Compute Swarm. Covers what the project is, how it is built, what is done, what is deliberately simplified, and what comes next.

## Purpose

Marine Compute Swarm is a locally served 3D web simulation of a swarm of floating, solar-powered compute nodes that track clouds and steer away from shade. It has two jobs:

1. **Investor demo.** The first screen leads with value: energy, compute, revenue and carbon offset for a daylight day, plus scale-up buttons (100 / 1,000 / 10,000 nodes). It must argue that the concept is realistic, has ROI, and scales across the planet.
2. **Design tool.** The founder can hover, click into an exploded view, and edit hardware to review and change the node design.

The first glance must stay simple. Every knob exists, but all of them live in a **Controls** drawer that starts closed.

## Hard rules

- The design must really float and stay stable. The editor never executes an edit that breaks hydrodynamic stability. It says "this would break hydrodynamic stability" and recommends which other knobs to change so the requested value works.
- The repo must not describe where it came from or which tools helped build it. Keep commit messages and docs about the product only.
- No agent is ever a co-author. Whichever agent writes the code, commits and pull requests carry no co-author trailer, no "generated by" line and no agent credit, even if a tool's defaults suggest adding one. The owner is the only author.
- Be honest in the numbers. Do not tune the model to flatter the product (see "Known findings").
- V1 means every major subsystem exists and works, not that it is optimal.

## Architecture

Three layers, dependencies pointing downward only.

1. **`src/model/`: pure TypeScript, no DOM, fully unit tested.** All randomness is a seeded mulberry32 RNG, so runs are reproducible.
   - `parts.ts`, `nodeDesign.ts`: a node is a list of parts (mass, position, size, power, cost, rationale), origin at keel bottom centre, y up. `workerDesign()` and `masterDesign()` derive their current mass from the parts list; see `evaluateWorst` and adds the Starlink terminal and mast, a second GNSS, a second battery and a second compute module. The hull is 1.60 m wide with 80 mm radiused corners and 8 tapered petals tilted 20° and 4 thrusters at 45° in an X layout.
   - `hydrostatics.ts`: floating checks, worst of deployed and stowed. Thresholds: reserve buoyancy ≥ 50%, GM ≥ 0.15 m, heel ≤ 10°, freeboard > 0, seawater 1025 kg/m³.
   - `solver.ts`: `applyEdit`. A requested knob change is accepted if stable (`ok`). Otherwise it searches up to 3 other knobs over a 9-point grid for the smallest normalized change that restores stability (`recommend`), else returns `infeasible` with a bisected `closest` value.
   - `power.ts`, `sky.ts`, `tracker.ts`, `navigator.ts`, `swarm.ts`, `impact.ts`, `config.ts`: power balance, wrapping 24 km sky with moving clouds and projected shadows, cloud tracking, navigation, swarm dynamics, and the scenario comparison (zod config).
2. **`src/state/`**: zustand `store.ts` (config, designs, selection, hover, exploded node, drawer, playback), `sim.ts` (the live `Sim` singleton: decides every 30 s, steps in sub-steps of at most 10 s), `editor.ts` (routes edits through the solver), `useImpact.ts` (debounced worker hook).
3. **`src/scene/` and `src/ui/`**: React Three Fiber scene and the HTML overlays. `src/workers/impact.worker.ts` runs the impact comparison off the main thread, with an inline fallback.

### Extension seams

- **`CloudTracker`** (`tracker.ts`): `simulatedTracker` adds noise and dropout. `samTrackerPlaceholder()` is where SAM 3.1 plugs in; it currently throws "SAM 3.1 not connected".
- **`Navigator`** (`navigator.ts`): `scriptedNavigator` (cloud dodging), `holdStationNavigator` (the baseline used for the comparison), and `agentNavigatorStub`. The stub is the seam for the planned agentic navigation harness, where a reasoning model drives navigation against the weather.

### Behavior worth knowing before editing

- Navigator commands are desired ground velocities. Thrust power is `0.5·ρ·Cd·A·v³/η`, and each node has a battery state of charge (100 Wh/kg).
- The impact comparison runs the scripted navigator and the hold-station baseline in the same weather. It covers 12 daylight hours in 30 s steps and is annualized ×365, which is an estimate.
- Hover ids are `nodeId` or `nodeId/partId`. Far node models are unmounted (`lod.ts`) because three's raycaster ignores `visible`, so hidden models would still catch pointer events.
- The exploded node is lifted 1.8 m above the water so no part is hidden by the ocean. The camera cannot dive below 15 m.
- The node camera picture-in-picture is an orthographic all-sky projection (north up, east right) with an SVG overlay of tracker boxes and arrows. Its `useFrame` has priority 1.
- Cloud puffs are billboarded in the vertex shader so they face any camera, not just the main one.
- Camera and selection are mirrored in the URL (`?cam=&target=&explode=`).
- In development, `window.__store` exposes the store for scripted checks.
- `src/setupTests.ts` mocks canvas `getContext` for jsdom.

## Known findings and simplifications

- **Dodging barely helps in the default scenario.** Scripted cloud-dodging gives about +0.8% energy and -1.1% compute against holding station, because thrust costs energy and clouds move faster than the nodes. This is shown as is. Default max speed is 0.4 m/s because 1.5 m/s burned all PV output. The agentic layer's longer-horizon positioning is the expected lever. Absolute output is healthy: about 210 kWh and $613 per day for 19 nodes.
- Cloud coverage is statistical: the realized shaded fraction varies by about ±0.1 by seed.
- Editing hull dimensions does not move other parts, so geometry can look odd after large hull edits.
- The Controls drawer's Hardware group edits the worker design only. Edited designs do feed the impact numbers and the live sim.
- Node visuals use meter-scale framed PV modules, hinges/struts, hollow shells, labeled battery packs, opened compute lids with representative modules in inspection, mounting hardware, cable/cooling routes and shrouded thrusters. Hardware envelopes remain estimates, not manufacturer CAD. The default equipment bays are checked for clearance; arbitrary hardware edits are gated for hydrostatics, not collision or packaging constraints. Stowing is still a simplified flat stack, not a validated folding mechanism.
- The sky camera is a flat all-sky projection, not a true fisheye. The ocean is opaque from below.
- Master failover and choosing scale-up regions are not modeled.
- Real-GPU performance has not been tested.
- `format.ts` and `urlState.ts` were written before their tests.

Design: `docs/design/2026-10-27-master-node-view-design.md`. Simulates only the master node: waves, hull motion, per-petal sun tracking, station keeping and a fisheye sky camera with a cloud-segmentation debug harness. It never steps the swarm.

- Pure model in `src/model/master/`; live instance `msim` and UI state in `src/state/master.ts`; scene in `src/scene/master/`; panels in `src/ui/master/`.
- Starting conditions via URL: `?view=master&mview=close|under|top|sky&tab=petals|propulsion|sky&az=95&el=12&sea=calm|swell|chop|storm&wind=3`.
- The fisheye sees the node's own petals and mast. East is on the image's left (upward-looking camera). Overlay ground truth is an analytic footprint disc, approximate for cumulus.
- Real SAM is not connected. Workflow: pause, Export frame (PNG + JSON), run a model offline, Import detections (`{"detections":[{"polygon":[[x,y]],"score":0.9}]}`). `samTrackerPlaceholder()` still throws.
- The master view's PV uses beam/diffuse irradiance on each tilted face, so it differs from the swarm model's flat area x GHI. It is shown side by side on purpose; `power.ts` was not changed.
- Findings: petals capture most of the gain; yaw assist is worth about 1% and the as-built radial thrusters have no yaw authority; wind drag dominates station keeping.
- Known simplifications: no petal-to-petal or mast shading, particle plumes are a Gaussian jet model not CFD, hull response constants are EST.

## Shared unit hardware update

- `src/model/hardware.ts` is the common source for L4 nominal dimensions and single-slot allowance, GPU-derived enclosure size, eight compass directions, paired drives, trapezoid geometry/centroid, octagonal deck area, and rounded hull section area/inertia.
- Swarm and unit views use the same `NodeModel` / `FacetPanel`. Hull wall corners are radiused 80 mm; equipment boxes use proportionate fillets. Full-size L4 cards are distinct parts, with their 72 W loads separate from the 136 W host allowance.
- Use `?view=unit&role=master|worker`; old `?view=master` still works. `useMaster.setRole` resets motion and changes the actual design. Shared store edits rebuild the unit model. Conditions and analysis panels are collapsible. `layoutRects` supplies rendering, picking and dimension-label coordinates for the same responsive viewport.
- Dimension inspection is available in both the swarm inspector and unit bench. The unit bench supports exploded inspection and Focus component; these pause physics. Camera presets frame the raised exploded assembly.
- Petal targets optimize paired irradiance, so E/SE, S/SW, W/NW, N/NE share tilt and rate limits. Ideal PV remains an independent-facet upper bound. Collecting area excludes panel borders; wind and mass use gross panel area. Hydrostatic mass positions use trapezoid centroids.
- GPU reference: NVIDIA PB-11316-001 v01 Fig. 4, 168.54 × 68.90 mm board; 20.32 mm is a slot allowance. Enclosure, host, cooling, actuator efficiency, linkage, and folding are estimates/concepts. Stock L4 system airflow and the proposed sealed cooling integration need thermal validation. No counterbalance credit or mechanically validated collision-free folding is claimed.
- Swarm actuator loads are 6 W average per pair (24 W total, estimated duty). Unit actuator telemetry estimates instantaneous gravity load at commanded rate plus motor overhead; it excludes wind/wave drive torque. Treat these as distinct assumptions.

- Impact throughput is capped by the `gpu-*` card power only (121 dense FP16 Tensor TFLOPS per 72 W L4; Ada whitepaper Appendix D). Host/coordinator power is overhead, not additional GPU throughput. Historical absolute compute/revenue findings above predate this correction and must be recomputed.

## Panel intersection correction

- `petalGeometry` now reserves swept edge space for any independent ±30° pose, rather than flat tiling. `FacetPanel` rounds the corners with quadratic curves (40 mm edge setbacks); the collecting area subtracts those same corner cutouts. Underside rails are inset within the collision envelope.
- `src/model/master/petalClearance.ts` checks all 28 panel pairs with convex-prism SAT, including glass/frame/rail thickness. `limitPetalMotion` certifies the whole angular path using a vertex-displacement bound and adaptive subdivision, with an 8 mm swept-clearance floor and a 12 mm operating margin that allows reversal away from a stop. It clips the complete linked-drive step before contact, preserves paired angles, and reports `collisionLimited` in telemetry. Do not bypass this check in the live simulation or check only the destination pose.
- Greater travel up to ±75° is conditional on the neighboring geometry. The analysis UI displays the clearance interlock; PV uses actual limited angles and stopped drives consume no modeled actuation power. An ideal independent-facet PV upper bound remains theoretical.
- Stowed layer pitch is 100 mm to clear the lower rails. This corrects static stack separation; it does not establish a physical folding sequence. Detailed linkage interference and self-shadowing remain unvalidated.

## Weather Simulator (`?view=weather`)

- Separate, responsive SVG fleet rehearsal page at `src/ui/weather/WeatherApp.tsx`, linked from Swarm and Unit bench. Seven resettable scenarios: Day, Night, Heavy winds, Storm, High swells, Tides and Current. Drag master/workers or use accessible injection buttons; conditions, playback speed, view extent, event log and design rationale are available.
- `src/model/weather.ts` is an independent deterministic conceptual model. It uses the current shared hardware designs for PV, fixed loads and battery capacity; it never steps either existing simulation or edits hardware. A through-water velocity controller plus estimated wind slip and sinusoidal current drives positions. Integrates in at most 1 s substeps. A zero-time refresh updates injected failure statuses while paused.
- A nominal **250 m/hop is a hypothesis**, not a tested radio range. Rain/wave radius penalties are uncalibrated heuristics. Connectivity flood-fills powered, upright, dry radios from the active master; this is not a link budget, packet simulation or throughput estimate.
- Proposed policy: retry mesh and hold cached formation slots; after 15 s without coordinator, return toward the cached slot. Flag retrieval when return makes no headway; shed compute below 25% battery and propulsion below 10%; the survival latch releases at 25%. Wildlife hold idles all thrusters and sheds compute. Master failure removes coordination and compute backhaul; low-rate satellite distress may still report to shore, with no leader election.
- Inversion and ingress are explicit injected failures. Inversion uses a hypothetical protected 15 W safety load and runs the conditional 60 s onboard righting state machine; radio is unavailable until upright. A failed kit stays inverted. Ingress isolates loads, charging and radio and remains latched until physical recovery. Reset represents physical recovery. The map always shows simulation truth, including positions that cannot be telemetered.
- Wave roll and rain are illustrative. No CFD, structural impact, wave-induced capsize prediction, full-angle righting curve, flooding dynamics, grounding, live weather or wildlife detection. Wind >=12 m/s requests survival stow and applies a PV/windage penalty; this is not a physical folding simulation. Static GM/checks shown are from existing hydrostatics and do not establish self-righting.
- The design discussion covers rounded upper buoyancy, low ballast, panel restraint, independent distress backhaul, wildlife response and further failure scenarios. These are proposed design directions, not installed hardware or validated safety guarantees.

## Live fleet recovery and automatic framing

- `weatherRecovery.ts` adds a conditional 60 s righting sequence to the actual fleet state: isolation, gas firing, inflation, roll, verification, upright. It runs locally without a coordinator link. A 0.1 Wh valve allowance is consumed from main power or protected beacon reserve, and the one-shot gas charge is spent. Failed/missing kit, ingress, no control power or a repeat capsize with spent gas leaves the node inverted and requests retrieval. This is an assumed mechanism outcome, not a solved full-angle stability or wave response. Bag remains deployed after success and needs servicing.
- Capsize injection stays on the 2D map and preserves playback state. Paused recovery explicitly asks the user to press Play. Map glyphs show the live righting angle, inflation and progress. The 3D bench can Follow live node using the same state, or independently replay the mechanism without changing fleet faults.
- Low charge enters survival mode below 10% and remains there until 25%. Three healthy workers form a 40 m nominal escort ring (radius limited by assumed radio range); up to three additional bridges cover initial separation. The coordinator and other nodes shift rendezvous to accompany drift, retaining mesh contact. Whole-fleet ring mode brings all healthy nodes around the survivor. Movement requires 35% responder reserve, adequate thrust margin, fresh reports and no other abandoned/damaged node; wildlife holds all thrusters. No physical enclosure, anchoring, wave attenuation, towing or energy transfer is credited.
- Weather time now cycles daily: Day starts at 10:00, Night at 18:00, sunset at 18:00 and sunrise at 06:00, with a sinusoidal 850 W/m² peak before weather losses. It is not a location-based forecast. Night uses the same mild baseline conditions as Day so the comparison isolates sunlight. 3600× playback makes an overnight drill reviewable without resetting conditions.
- `fleetMap.ts` continuously frames every node with symbol/label padding and equal x/y meter scale. The grid stays anchored in world coordinates with a dynamic meter spacing and scale bar. There are no manual extent, reset-fit or offscreen-node controls. Dragging captures the initial camera transform so auto-fit cannot feed back into pointer displacement.
- Regression tests cover both roles' righting, spent and failed kits, overnight escort drift/contact/solar recovery, whole-fleet formation, camera bounds and live 3D synchronization.
